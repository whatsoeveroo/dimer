Dimer V2 build plan

A second, smaller Dimer. Same idea as V1: two tracks, a face, table-edge sensing, a front shovel and voice. The head and shovel are fixed, there is no custom board, and it runs on one flat battery. V1 stays as it is.

How it works

What is inside

One small computer, the ESP32-S3 Super Mini, runs everything. The charger board both charges the battery from USB-C and turns it into 5 V. Every other part connects to the brain with plug-in jumper wires.

flowchart LR
  USB[USB-C phone charger] --> CHG[Charger and 5 V board]
  BAT[1S LiPo 3.7 V] <--> CHG
  CHG --> SW[Slide switch] --> V5[5 V line]
  V5 --> ESP[ESP32-S3 brain]
  V5 --> DRV[Motor driver] --> M[Two track motors]
  V5 --> AMP[Amplifier] --> SPK[Speaker]
  ESP --> DRV
  ESP --> AMP
  ESP --> LCD[Face display]
  EDGE[2 table-edge sensors] --> ESP
  TOF[Distance sensor] --> ESP
  MIC[Microphone] --> ESP
  ESP <-- Wi-Fi --> AI[OpenAI API: hear, think, speak]

Assembly

Putting it together, step by step

Tap a step to open or close it. Step 2 is the only one with solder.

0Lay out the parts and toolsCheck every part against the buying list before you start.
  • Tools: a small cross-head screwdriver for the M2 screws, a soldering iron for the six power joints in step 2, scissors, double-sided foam tape, and tape for labelling wires.
  • Keep the battery unplugged from everything until step 2 says otherwise.
  • The printed frame is a rough guideline. Several boards are held with foam tape for now. Each step says so where that is the case.
1Fit the two drive motorsOne motor at the rear left, one at the front right, each shaft out through its side wall.
  • Two N20 motors are too long to sit side by side in a 34 mm wide body, so they sit at opposite corners. The rear one drives the left track, the front one drives the right track.
  • Push each gearbox into its corner so the shaft comes out through the 4.6 mm hole. The small rib next to each motor holds it square.
  • A strip of foam tape under each motor stops it from rattling.
  • Label the cables L and R. Left and right are as seen from behind the robot, looking forward.
2Wire and place the power partsBattery on the floor, charger board on top of it at the rear, with its USB-C socket in the rear wall slot.
  • This is the only step with solder: about six joints. Battery red to the charger's B+ pad, battery black to B-. Two wires from the charger's 5 V output: one goes to the switch, the other side of the switch becomes the 5 V line for everything else. A third wire from the charger's GND becomes the shared ground.
  • If you would rather not solder, a maker space or a phone repair shop can do these six joints in a few minutes.
  • Stick the battery to the floor with foam tape, wires toward the rear. Run a strap through the two floor slots if it moves.
  • Stick the charger board on top of the battery at the rear, USB-C socket pushed into the slot in the rear wall.
  • Measure your battery first. It must be no bigger than 40 × 30 × 10 mm.
3Stick down the boardsThe brain, motor driver and amplifier sit on top of the battery and motors.
  • Motor driver at the rear right, brain in the middle, amplifier toward the front. All three on foam tape, pins facing up.
  • The brain's USB-C socket faces the rear so you can reach it with the lid off for programming.
  • Leave about 16 mm above each board: the jumper wire plugs need that room under the lid.
4Fit the distance sensorIt looks forward through the small window in the front wall, just under the lid.
  • Plug its four wires on first (VIN, GND, SCL, SDA), then press the sensor against the inside of the front wall with the lens in the window.
  • Hold it with foam tape for now. Its two mounting holes line up with nothing yet.
5Fit the shovel and the edge sensorsThe edge sensors screw under the shovel shelf, then the shovel screws to the front wall.
  • Plug three jumper wires onto each sensor first: VCC, GND, DO. Use the same three colours on both sensors.
  • Fix each sensor under the shelf with one M3 self-tapping screw through its board hole, black sensor head looking down through the square opening.
  • Lead the wires in through the notch in the front wall, then fix the shovel with two M2 screws from the front.
  • The shovel does not move. It is part of the body, like a bumper.
6Build the head into the lidDisplay behind the window, speaker behind the grille, microphone under the small top hole.
  • Display: plug its wires on first, then tape it behind the front window, screen facing out.
  • Speaker: cone facing the grille in the back of the head, held by a ring of foam tape around the rim. Do not press on the cone.
  • Microphone: tape it under the hole in the head roof, with the sound hole on its board facing up.
  • Power switch: push it up through the slot in the lid behind the head so the lever sticks out on top.
  • All their wires drop down through the big opening under the head.
7Plug in every wireEach jumper wire goes from a part to a brain pin. The wiring table below lists every one.
  • Work through the wiring table one row at a time and tick it off.
  • Every 5 V wire comes from the switched 5 V line, every GND wire from the shared ground. A short strip of jumper wires joined with a 2.54 mm pin header makes a tidy splitter.
  • Read the printed pin names twice before pushing each plug on. A sensor plugged in backwards can be damaged.
  • Close the lid: two M2 screws at the rear, two through the front tabs.
8Fit the tracksPrinted wheels on the axles, then a rubber band or printed TPU belt around each pair.
  • On each side one wheel presses onto the motor's 3 mm D-shaft. The other is an idler that turns on an M3 screw in the small hole.
  • Stretch two O-rings or one TPU belt around each pair of wheels. The band should be snug without bending the wheels inward.
  • The wheel sizes are in the frame script. Print a test pair first and adjust the bore if the press fit is too loose or too tight.
9First power-onSwitch on, look for the light on the brain, then charge through the USB-C slot.
  • Switch on. A small light on the ESP32 board should come on within a second.
  • If nothing lights, or anything gets warm or smells, switch off straight away, unplug the battery and tell me what you saw.
  • To charge: plug a phone charger into the USB-C slot in the rear wall. The charger board's light shows when it is full. Never leave a charging battery unattended.
  • After this the next job is the first test program.

This uses all 15 free pins on the Super Mini, so there is no spare pin for a button or light later without dropping something.
